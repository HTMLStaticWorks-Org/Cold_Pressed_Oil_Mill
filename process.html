The Art of Cold-Pressed Extraction

Our 6-Step Traditional Chekku Journey

At MARACHEKKU NATURALS, oil-making is an unhurried craft guided by sunlight, seasoned vagai timber, and natural gravity. Walk through the six stages every batch undergoes before reaching your kitchen.

Step 01 of 06

Raw Seed Selection

Because traditional cold-pressed oil is never refined or deodorised, the flavour of the finished bottle depends entirely on the soundness of the raw seeds. We source mature, seasonal harvests of dryland groundnuts, black and white sesame seeds, mature coconuts, mustard seeds, and castor beans.

  • Single-variety seed lots kept strictly separate to preserve true varietal character.
  • Groundnuts kept in protective shells until shortly before pressing day.
  • Sulfur-free, sun-dried mature coconut halves selected for coconut oil batches.
Step 02 of 06

Cleaning & Courtyard Sun-Preparation

Before entering the mill room, seeds are winnowed in traditional woven bamboo trays (muram) and multi-mesh sieves to remove field dust, stems, stones, and shrivelled kernels. The cleaned seeds and split copra are then spread across clean woven mats under natural morning sunlight.

  • Manual and sieve cleaning eliminates foreign husk fragments that cause bitterness.
  • Natural courtyard sun-warming removes excess surface moisture without roasting.
  • Ensures clean binding of the seed cake (punnakku) during pressing.
Step 03 of 06

Traditional Vagai-Wood Chekku Extraction

Prepared seeds are fed into our carved wooden mortar (ural) fitted with a heavy seasoned hardwood pestle (ulakkai). Rotating at a slow, steady pace of 14 to 16 revolutions per minute, the wooden pestle gently crushes the seeds against the wooden chamber walls at ambient temperature.

  • Seasoned Vagai wood naturally insulates the seeds against friction heat buildup.
  • For traditional Nallennai (sesame oil), a small piece of natural palm jaggery is added to aid pressing.
  • Fresh golden oil trickles down the wooden channel without steam or chemical solvents.
Step 04 of 06

Natural Settling & Cotton Cloth Filtration

Freshly pressed chekku oil emerges cloudy with microscopic seed fibres. Rather than forcing the oil through chemical bleaching clays or high-pressure synthetic filter pads, we strain it through unbleached gada cotton cloth into food-grade stainless steel and brass settling drums.

  • Rested undisturbed for 48 to 72 hours in a warm, sunlit settling room.
  • Natural gravity gently draws fine seed particles to the bottom of the drum.
  • The clarified upper golden layer is decanted while preserving natural seed waxes and aroma.
Step 05 of 06

Careful Batch & Sensory Quality Checking

Before any batch is cleared for bottling, our mill supervisors conduct a practical sensory and visual inspection. Each lot is checked in a tall glass cylinder against natural daylight to verify proper sedimentation, characteristic colour, and clean varietal aroma.

  • Sensory verification of authentic nutty or sweet seed aroma with zero mustiness.
  • Inspection of the pressed oilseed cake (punnakku) for proper dryness and pressing consistency.
  • Each batch is recorded in our mill register with pressing date and seed batch lot code.
Step 06 of 06

Hygienic Bottling & Bulk Tin Packaging

Once clarified and inspected, the oil is filled in our clean dispatch room into inert 250 ml, 500 ml, and 1 L glass bottles for household kitchens, as well as light-proof 5 L and 15 L food-grade tin containers for restaurants, caterers, and wholesale buyers.

  • Tamper-evident caps and protective inner seals prevent leakage and air exposure.
  • Clearly labelled with oil variety, extraction method, pressing batch code, and storage care.
  • Dispatched directly from our mill to households and trade buyers.
Zero-Waste Mill Tradition

Nothing Wasted: Traditional Oil Cake (Punnakku)

Because our wooden chekku presses operate without chemical solvents or petroleum hexane, the solid seed cake left behind after pressing—known in Tamil as punnakku—remains completely natural and chemical-free.

Groundnut, sesame, and coconut cakes from our mill are supplied directly to local dairy farmers as traditional cattle fodder, while neem and mahua seed cakes are used by regional growers for natural soil enrichment.
